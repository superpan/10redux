# Where this stack would have been in 2016

A ten-equivalent in mid-2016 looks nothing like ten today — almost every piece either didn't exist or was wildly less capable. This doc maps the current stack onto its closest 2016 analogue, characterises the quality bar of that era, and names the *structural unlock* that made caption-mediated retrieval viable.

The point isn't nostalgia. It's that the architectural choices in ten — "caption, then text-search; no retrieval-specific training; off-the-shelf models throughout" — are downstream of a model convergence that happened ~2022–2023. Before that, this design was not just worse, it was *not buildable*.

## Layer-by-layer comparison

| layer | ten (2026) | closest 2016 equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Visual encoder | V-JEPA 2 ViT-L (self-supervised, native temporal) | ResNet-50/152 ImageNet features per frame; **C3D** for short clips (Tran et al., ICCV 2015); two-stream RGB + optical flow (Simonyan & Zisserman, NIPS 2014) |
| Aggregation over time | V-JEPA's spatio-temporal tokens | mean/max-pool, **NetVLAD** (2016), or LSTM over per-frame features (LRCN, S2VT) |
| Captioner | Qwen3-VL-8B, prompt-able, 256K context, factual sentences | **S2VT** (Venugopalan 2015) or Show-Attend-Tell-style CNN+LSTM (Xu, ICML 2015). Trained per-domain on MSR-VTT/MSVD; ~10–20 BLEU; couldn't follow prompts; hallucinated heavily; no "describe this 10 s clip in 1–2 factual sentences" capability. In practice: most production systems just used user-uploaded titles/descriptions and skipped video captioning entirely. |
| Text embedder | Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (BERT-descendant, instruction-tuned) | **word2vec** averaged, **GloVe**, FastText (July 2016), or Skip-Thought sentence vectors. No BERT until Oct 2018. Sentence retrieval quality dropped off a cliff for anything beyond keyword overlap. |
| ASR | Whisper-large-v3 (multilingual, 100+ languages, robust to wind/music) | **DeepSpeech 2** (Baidu, Dec 2015) or **LAS** (Aug 2016); Microsoft hit human parity on Switchboard in Oct 2016. **Per-domain training required** — no general-purpose model. Open-source defaults were Kaldi GMM-HMM/DNN-HMM. Multilingual coverage was bespoke. |
| Audio embeddings | LAION CLAP (text-audio contrastive) | **VGGish** (Hershey et al., 2017 — barely 2016). AudioSet labels released March 2017. **No text↔audio contrastive model existed** — you trained a CNN on spectrograms for sound *classification* and retrieved by tag, not by free-text query. |
| Vector store | Qdrant (HNSW, payload-rich, REST) | **Annoy** (Spotify, 2013); **HNSW** paper just out (Malkov & Yashunin, March 2016) — no production-grade managed product. **Faiss** open-source landed March 2017. Most teams ran brute-force or wrote their own kd-tree/LSH. Pinecone / Milvus / Weaviate / Qdrant all post-2019. |
| Reranking | Qwen3-Reranker cross-encoder | Linear SVM or learning-to-rank (LambdaMART, XGBoost) over hand-crafted text+visual features. |
| Retrieval architecture | Caption-as-bridge: LLM writes free-form text, text-vector search over it | **Joint visual-semantic embedding** (DeViSE-style, Karpathy & Fei-Fei 2015, "Word2VisualVec" Dong 2016). Train a CNN-side and a text-side projection into a shared space. The "caption it then text-search" approach was impossible because captioners couldn't produce queryable prose. |
| Multimodal Q&A | Qwen3-VL re-decodes the clip for summaries | VQA models (VQA dataset 2015) — small custom networks, fixed-answer-space, no free-form descriptions. |

## Quality bar in 2016

MSR-VTT 1K-A R@1 ballpark (from the paper's own baselines + immediate follow-ups, 2016–early 2017):

- VSE / Word2VisualVec / Mean-pool baselines: **R@1 ≈ 4–12 %**
- Best published end-to-end (C+LSTM+SA+FC7 variants): **~12 %**
- **ten today: 0.36** caption-mediated, no fine-tuning
- **2024+ end-to-end SOTA: 0.43 – 0.55**

A 2016 build of "ten" wouldn't be a worse ten — it'd be a fundamentally different and structurally weaker product. The "caption it, then search captions" architecture was *not viable*. The default would have been a **joint embedding head trained from scratch on MSR-VTT-style pairs**, returning a top-5 across mostly-correct genres and frequently-wrong specifics.

## What you would have built in 2016 instead

If you'd been asked to ship "ten" in mid-2016, the realistic system:

1. **Per-frame ResNet-50 features at 1 fps**, mean-pooled into one 2048-d vector per video clip.
2. **NetVLAD or LSTM aggregation** if you wanted to be fancier and had time.
3. **No captioning.** Index user-uploaded titles + tags as keyword text. Maybe S2VT for "auto-tag" if you had researchers.
4. **Word2vec averaged for the text side**, projected into a 256-d joint space via DeViSE-style margin loss trained on whatever pairs you could scrape.
5. **Annoy or a custom inverted-file LSH** for ANN; brute force at < 100 k items.
6. **No ASR** unless your library was specifically dialogue-heavy and English-only — and then you'd train your own DeepSpeech variant.
7. **No audio embeddings.** Tag-based audio retrieval was the ceiling.
8. **Faceted / keyword search UI**, not "describe what you remember" prose queries — because the prose-query path didn't have an encoder that could handle paraphrase.

A team of 6–10 people for ~12 months would get you something usable on a narrow domain. Reusing it for a new library meant retraining the joint embedding head from scratch.

## The structural unlock

The unlock that makes ten possible isn't any single model — it's the **stack convergence around 2022–2023**:

- **Prompt-able vision-language models** that can produce queryable prose from arbitrary video. Without this, captions are either non-existent or domain-bound.
- **General-purpose ASR** (Whisper, 2022) that works across languages and conditions with zero fine-tuning. Pre-2022, ASR was per-language, per-domain, per-acoustic-environment work.
- **Text encoders that handle paraphrase semantics**. BERT (Oct 2018) was the first real step; sentence-transformers (2019) made it accessible; instruction-tuned embedders (2024) made it stable across query styles.
- **Managed HNSW vector stores** (Qdrant 2020, Pinecone 2019). Before this, "search a million vectors" was a research project.

None of those existed in 2016. The closest you could get back then would have scored ~12 % R@1 on MSR-VTT, required a team of 6–10 people for a year, and been unable to handle free-form natural-language queries at all.

## One-sentence framing

> **Caption-as-bridge retrieval is post-LLM. Pre-2020 the only viable text ↔ video retrieval was joint-embedding training, which requires paired data and doesn't generalize across domains.**

That's why a single-person side project gets to where it does today, and why the same project was out of reach in 2016 even for well-resourced teams.
